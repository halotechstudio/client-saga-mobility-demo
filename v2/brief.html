1What's in this release

An interactive simulation of the four apps SAGA asked for, built on one design system that will drop into SAGA's existing Flutter app:

  • Rider app, in an iPhone build and an Android build, with light and dark mode, English / తెలుగు / हिन्दी, and the Saathi voice assistant on every screen.
  • Chauffeur app (Android, company phones).
  • SAGA for Hotels, a tablet and web app for partner hotels to book for guests and see their commission.
  • Ops dashboard: fleet, waitlist, flight watch, hotel partners, offers and notification templates.
  • The screen map, the end-to-end schema drawn with the live screens, and this brief.

What changed since the first concept

  • Logo and colour are SAGA's own. Our earlier logo is gone. Until the files arrive, the apps use a neutral wordmark and a placeholder blue. Paste SAGA's hex code and upload the logo on the suite page and every app re-themes, in light and dark, with text contrast checked.
  • No 3D ride animation. During a ride the rider sees a regular map in SAGA's colours: the route, the car marker and the ETA.
  • The car is the Mahindra XUV 3XO EV, presented as the premium product it is: on the welcome screen, on Home, in the ride sheet and on its own "The car" page.
  • An honest message when no car is free, offering a place in line, a scheduled ride or a notification.
  • Different iPhone and Android builds, a hotel partner app, dark mode, three languages and voice.

3Airport rides from a PNR

The honest versionNo Indian airline offers a public API that lets a third party look up a booking from a PNR and surname. Airline "Manage booking" pages accept PNR and surname from the passenger, and travel-agency (GDS) access only covers bookings that agency made. So "type the PNR and it schedules itself" has to be built in two steps: identify the flight, then track it.

Step A: find the flight, ideally without the rider typing anything

  1. Catch the booking as it arrives (§4). Android reads the airline's SMS or app alert through notification access. iPhone passes airline messages to SAGA through a Shortcuts automation. On both, a personal SAGA travel address receives every airline email through a one-time mail filter. Each route delivers the full ticket text: PNR, airline, flight, date and passenger names.
  2. WhatsApp forward. Many Indian airlines and travel sites now send tickets on WhatsApp. The rider forwards the message or PDF to SAGA's WhatsApp number, and the chat replies with the pickup time and a Book button.
  3. Calendar match. Gmail and the airlines add flights to the calendar, so SAGA finds them with calendar permission alone.
  4. PNR typed by hand. We ask for the airline and date. SAGA serves one airport, so one airline's departures on one day is a short list of about 5–15 flights. The rider taps theirs and the PNR is stored with it.
  5. Partnerships (later). An airline or travel site adds a "Book your SAGA" button that sends us the PNR and flight details. This is a business-development deal, not an API we can call today.
Not recommended: looking the PNR up on airline websitesAutomating airline "Manage booking" pages with a PNR and surname breaks their terms of use and runs into CAPTCHAs. It also handles exactly the data that exposed passengers' contact details in past airline incidents. Every route above uses data the rider chose to share.

Step B: track the flight and time the pickup

Pickup time = departure − airport buffer − drive time at that hour. The default buffers are 90 minutes for domestic flights and 180 for international; SAGA sets them. Drive time comes from the Google Routes API with traffic for that time of day. The rule is stored, not the time, so every flight update recalculates the pickup. If a flight moves by 10 minutes or more, the pickup moves and the rider is told. A cancellation, or a delay over 60 minutes, asks the rider before reassigning. For arrivals, the chauffeur is at the kerb 10 minutes after landing, with 45 minutes of free waiting.

4Catching flight bookings automatically

The client wants SAGA to notice a flight booking as soon as it's made and schedule the ride. Neither phone lets an app read SMS or email directly. But three routes get the same result automatically after a one-time set-up, and two more need only one tap.

A · Android: flight alerts through notification access

Airline SMS, airline-app alerts and WhatsApp messages all appear as notifications. With the rider's permission, SAGA's notification listener sees them as they arrive. Unlike SMS read access, this isn't one of Google Play's restricted permissions.

  1. In onboarding (and in Settings), the rider turns on "Flight alerts". SAGA first explains, in plain words, exactly what it reads. Then Android's own Notification access screen opens.
  2. On the phone, SAGA ignores every notification except those from airline SMS senders, airline and travel apps, and WhatsApp messages that match a ticket pattern (PNR + flight number + date).
  3. Only the extracted flight fields go to SAGA's server. The notification text never leaves the phone.
  4. The flight is checked with the flight-status API, and the rider gets "Book a pickup at 5:20 AM?", or an automatic booking if they chose auto-book.

Conditions: Play's User Data policy requires the in-app disclosure and consent above, and use of the access for this feature only. Google can still ask about it at review, so the other routes remain as a fallback. Android 15 and later hide one-time-password notifications from listeners; flight alerts aren't affected. Some phone brands stop background listeners to save battery, so SAGA guides the rider through excluding it once.

B · iPhone: a Shortcuts automation

iOS gives apps no access to messages, but the rider's own Shortcuts app can pass a message to SAGA. SAGA ships an App Intent, "Find a flight in this text", and a 1-minute guided set-up:

  1. Shortcuts → Automation → Message → "Message contains: PNR" → Run immediately.
  2. Action: SAGA · Find a flight in this text, with the message as input. SAGA provides a ready-made shortcut link, so this is one tap.
  3. The same set-up with the Email trigger catches tickets in Apple Mail.

Apps can't create automations themselves, so the rider does this once with on-screen steps. It runs on iOS 17 and later without asking each time.

C · Both phones: a personal travel address

  1. Every rider gets a private address, for example ananya.7q2@trips.sagamobility.com. Mail sent there is matched to that rider, whichever account it was forwarded from.
  2. A one-time Gmail or Outlook filter forwards mail from airlines and travel sites (IndiGo, Air India, Akasa, SpiceJet, MakeMyTrip, ixigo, and others) to that address. Gmail asks to confirm a new forwarding address; SAGA's server confirms it automatically. Gmail filters are set up in a browser, and SAGA shows a 1-minute guide.
  3. From then on every booking reaches SAGA on its own, including bookings made for family members, which SAGA asks about before booking.

This gets the same emails as the Gmail API without Google's restricted-scope review or the yearly security assessment.

D · Both phones: forward on WhatsApp

The rider forwards the ticket message or PDF to SAGA's WhatsApp Business number. A bot reads it and replies in the same chat, in the rider's language: "6E 2134, Fri 20 Nov, 7:40 AM. Pickup from Home at 5:20 AM, ₹1,140. [Book] [Change time]". It works even before the rider installs the app. Under Meta's current pricing, replies within 24 hours of the rider's message aren't charged by Meta; the WhatsApp provider's platform fee applies.

E · Calendar, on the phone or from Google

Device calendar access finds flights when the app opens. For background updates, the Google Calendar API can watch the rider's calendar for flight events Gmail adds. Calendar is a "sensitive" scope, not a restricted one, so it needs Google's app verification but no paid yearly assessment. Gmail adds these events only when its smart features are on, so this route complements the others.

From a found flight to a booked ride

  1. One parser handles every source: SMS text, email, PDF, WhatsApp message. Template rules cover the common airlines; an AI model (for example Claude Haiku) handles the rest for a fraction of a rupee. It extracts PNR, airline, flight, date and time, route and passenger names, with a confidence score. Low-confidence results are shown to the rider rather than booked.
  2. Both legs: a departure from Vizag becomes a drop to the airport; an arrival into Vizag becomes a pickup at Arrivals.
  3. Whose trip? If the passenger isn't the account holder, for example tickets booked for parents, SAGA asks "Book this ride for Mr. Rao?" and sends the chauffeur details to the traveller.
  4. Auto-book (opt-in): SAGA books with the usual pickup rule and sends a confirmation with a one-tap cancel, free until a chauffeur is assigned. Without auto-book, the rider gets a one-tap suggestion.
  5. The original message or email is deleted once the flight is saved, and the PNR is stored encrypted until the trip ends.

Cost: notification access, Shortcuts and calendar are free. Inbound email and AI parsing cost a fraction of a rupee per ticket. WhatsApp costs the provider's platform fee.

5Scheduled rides on repeat

  • A series is stored as a calendar rule (RFC 5545 RRULE: days, time, end date) plus skipped dates. The backend creates real bookings 14 days ahead so dispatch can plan cars and charging.
  • Riders can skip a day free of charge until 8 PM the evening before (configurable), add a return ride, and ask for the same chauffeur. Dispatch tries to honour that every day.
  • Pricing: per ride, or as a weekday plan with a discount. Plans can be paid monthly through a UPI Autopay mandate from the payment gateway.
  • Reminders go out the evening before. Each series appears in Trips as one card, with Skip next and Manage.

6When no car is free

  1. When a rider books, the backend looks for free cars and for cars about to become free: those finishing a trip, and those charged enough to leave the hub.
  2. If the best pickup ETA is above a threshold (suggested: 8 minutes), the rider sees the honest screen. It says SAGA is a young company growing its fleet, gives the real wait, and promises a premium ride that makes it worth it. It's written in all three languages.
  3. The rider can wait in line, which locks the fare and earns 50 points. They can also schedule, or ask to be notified when a car is free.
  4. Ops sees the waitlist on Command and can release a car from charging or offer a scheduled slot.
  5. Measure how many waitlisted riders still ride. This tells SAGA when to add cars and in which areas.

8Running offers

  • Offer types: % off with a cap, flat ₹ off, SAGA points, free waiting time.
  • Triggers SAGA can target: first airport ride · a flight detected in the next 14 days · scheduled plans with 20+ rides · referrals · partner-hotel guests · riders who waited for a car · riders inactive for 30 days · festivals.
  • Guardrails: one offer per ride (the best one wins), a uses-per-rider limit, a total budget that auto-stops at 100%, and de-duplication by device, phone number and UPI ID to stop abuse.
  • Process: ops drafts an offer in the dashboard, finance approves the budget, and it goes live on schedule. Results show redemptions, budget used and repeat rides. Keep a small holdout group to measure real lift.
  • Eligible offers apply automatically at quote time, so riders don't hunt for codes. Codes stay available for campaigns. GST on discounted fares to be confirmed by SAGA's CA.

9Hotel partners and how the money moves

A hotel books a ride for a guest from SAGA for Hotels. The guest doesn't need the app: they get the chauffeur's details and a tracking link on WhatsApp. The hotel earns a commission on every ride it books. There are three ways a ride can be paid for:

ModeWho collects the fareHow commission is paidGood for
A · Room billThe hotel, on the guest's folioNetted off in the monthly statementMost hotel guests
B · Guest pays SAGASAGA, via a UPI link sent to the guest on WhatsAppNetted off, or paid out monthlyWalk-in requests, budget hotels
C · Hotel accountBilled to the hotel (VIPs, packages)Netted off in the monthly statementComplimentary transfers
Optional · Split at sourceSAGA, through the payment gateway's split-payment productThe gateway transfers the commission per rideHotels that want daily settlement

Recommended: one monthly statement that nets everything

On the 1st, SAGA issues each hotel a statement: fares the hotel collected (A + C), minus commission on all rides (A + B + C), with GST on the commission and TDS. The hotel pays the net amount by the 7th. In a month where commission exceeds what the hotel collected, SAGA pays the difference. Example from the demo, Bay Pavilion, November:

Fares collected by hotel (room bill ₹96,300 + hotel account ₹16,500)₹1,12,800
Commission, 10% of all 142 rides (₹1,84,200)− ₹18,420
GST on commission, 18%, from the hotel's commission invoice− ₹3,316
TDS deducted by SAGA on commission, 2%+ ₹368

Tax and legal points for SAGA's CA
  • GST on passenger transport by cab (shown at 5%), and whether the room bill is treated as the hotel collecting on SAGA's behalf.
  • The hotel's commission is its service to SAGA, invoiced with GST if the hotel is registered.
  • TDS on commission under Section 194H: 2% since 1 Oct 2024, with a ₹20,000 yearly threshold from April 2025. It is renumbered under the new Income-tax Act from FY 2026-27.
  • A partner agreement covering rates, statement dates, cancellations, guest-data handling under the DPDP Act 2023, and branding.

Onboarding a hotel: KYC (GSTIN, PAN, bank account) → partner agreement → staff logins → a 30-minute walkthrough → a QR stand at reception. Guests can also scan the QR to book themselves, with the hotel's code attached so the hotel still earns its commission.

10Voice in Telugu, Hindi and English

There are two layers. The first does everything; the second gives fast shortcuts from outside the app.

Layer 1 · Saathi, inside the app (every phone, every feature)

  1. The rider taps the mic: the circle next to the tab bar on iPhone, or the Saathi button on Android. They speak in English, Telugu, Hindi, or a mix.
  2. Streaming speech-to-text turns speech into text. Options: Sarvam AI (Indian-language specialist; ₹30 per hour of audio list price) or Google Cloud Speech / the Gemini Live API.
  3. An AI model with tool-calling maps the request to app actions (book, airport, schedule, intercity, find my car, offers, settings) and fills in details: "office every weekday at 9" becomes a weekday series at 9:00 AM.
  4. The app shows what it understood, asks for a confirmation before anything is booked or paid, and replies in text and speech in the same language.

The simulation shows this flow: open the mic on any screen, or use the suggestions. In Chrome it also listens for real, in all three languages. Cost: a typical 6-second command costs about ₹0.05 for speech-to-text, plus a few paise of AI-model usage.
